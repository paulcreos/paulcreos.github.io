Raven Warden II continues the roundel series with a different balance of density and open space. The raven faces outward with its wings extended, while the triskele anchors the composition and gives the eye a point of return.

The surrounding knotwork is more than a frame. Its continuous lines suggest an unbroken boundary, echoing the raven’s place between the visible world and the Otherworld. The piece draws on the raven’s layered meanings as witness, messenger, and keeper of memory. These themes are explored in [The Raven in Celtic Mythology and Tradition — Between Battlefield, Memory, and the Otherworld](/articles/the-raven-in-celtic-mythology-and-tradition), the accompanying article about the raven in Celtic tradition.

## Within the boundary

The central bird is carved as a presence that observes before it acts. Its wings create a protective span, sheltering the open triskele that turns through its three arms as a mark of continuous movement and return.

The birch surface keeps the work close to the character of the material. Grain, tool marks, and the contrast between the lighter relief and darker ground reveal the image naturally in the wood. The circular form gives the raven a quiet authority, suited to a wall, an entrance, or a personal place of reflection.

The piece brings ancient Celtic motifs into conversation with the natural grain and weight of seasoned birch. It finds its full meaning in the quiet space it is chosen to inhabit.

The roundel belongs to a private collection.
