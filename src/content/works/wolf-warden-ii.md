Wolf Warden II carries the same guardian spirit into a tighter, more concentrated composition. The wolf turns into the circle with its teeth visible and its eye fixed on the space beyond the frame. Around it, knotwork forms an unbroken boundary, while the chain and small pendants add weight to the figure’s sense of duty.

The relief is not an image of a tame animal. Its force comes from the tension between the wild head and the deliberate symbols attached to it. The chain may suggest restraint, but it can also be read as a bond, an oath, or a responsibility accepted by the one who stands watch. The triangular knot and triskele bring different rhythms into the composition without softening the wolf’s alert presence.

The wider meaning of the wolf as a creature of wilderness, transformation, protection, and uncertain thresholds is explored in [The Wolf in Celtic Mythology and Tradition — Between the Wild and the Human](/articles/the-wolf-in-celtic-mythology-and-tradition). This roundel is a contemporary symbolic work shaped by those stories, by observation of nature, and by the character of carved wood; it is not a reconstruction of a historical artefact.

## Strength under responsibility

Birch gives the piece a warm, responsive surface. The raised carving catches the light against the darker ground, making the fur, teeth, chain, and knotwork legible from a distance while preserving the marks of the hand up close. The round format turns the wolf’s gaze into a direct encounter: the viewer meets the guardian at the edge of its territory.

Wolf Warden II belongs to the same family of threshold images as its predecessor, but its mood is more immediate. It feels less like a quiet sentinel and more like a warning that has already been heard. The wolf does not need to chase what approaches. It only needs to remain present, know the boundary, and decide what may pass.

The roundel belongs to a private collection.

May this warden bring courage without aggression, instinct without fear, and the strength to protect what deserves to remain within the circle.
